**Scope:** The physical nature of light before it enters the human eye or interacts with paint — electromagnetic duality, wavelength/energy quantization, prismatic dispersion, emission vs. reflection, refraction and scattering, standard illuminants and correlated color temperature, and the spectral-locus boundaries of physical color space.

### [[Wave Nature|Light/Wave Nature]]
Light is **electromagnetic radiation**: coupled electric and magnetic fields that oscillate transversally, perpendicular to their direction of travel. It exhibits **wave–particle dualism** — described as a vibrating disturbance spread in space in the wave model, or as discrete, indivisible packets of energy called **photons** in the particle model. In a vacuum, light travels at the constant universal speed **c ≈ 300,000 km/s**. It is quantified by wavelength (λ, the distance between crests, in nanometers), frequency (the number of waves per second), and energy (per Planck's law, in eV: **E(eV) = 1240/λ(nm)**). Physical intensity or brightness is the flux — the number of photons passing a unit area in a unit time (*The Science of Paintings*, *Vision Science: Photons to Phenomenology*, *Illusions of Seeing*).

### [[The Visible Spectrum|Light/The Visible Spectrum]]
The visible spectrum is the narrow portion of the electromagnetic spectrum that the eye perceives, from about **380–400 nm to 700–780 nm**. Newton's prism experiment established that "white" sunlight is a **heterogeneous mixture** of component wavelengths, which the prism refracts at different angles into a continuous sequence of spectral hues — the colors are components, not modifications, of white light. Some perceived colors, notably **purple/magenta**, have no single corresponding wavelength: they are physical mixtures of red and violet (*The Science of Paintings*, *Measuring Colour*).

### [[Reflection vs. Emission|Light/Reflection vs. Emission]]
Light that is directly emitted from a source — the sun, flames, electrical filaments — is **radiant light**. Heated solids emit a predictable **blackbody** spectrum; by Wien's law the peak wavelength shifts to shorter values as temperature rises. Light can also be **reflected**: smooth, polished surfaces give **specular** reflection (angle of incidence equals angle of reflection), while dull or matte surfaces scatter light **diffusely** in many directions. Matter also **absorbs** selected wavelengths (photon energy transfers to the material, often as heat) and **refracts** light as it changes speed between media; **fluorescence** absorbs high-energy photons and re-emits lower-energy (longer-wavelength) photons (*The Science of Paintings*, *Vision Science: Photons to Phenomenology*, *Contemporary Color*).

### [[Illuminants & Correlated Color Temperature|Light/Illuminants & Correlated Color Temperature]]
Every source is defined physically by its **spectral power distribution (SPD)** — power emitted at each wavelength. **Illuminants** are the mathematical, international descriptions of a light source used in colorimetry: **A** (incandescent tungsten, ~2856 K), **C** (filtered daylight), **D65** ("average" daylight, 6500 K; preferred over C because it includes proper UV), and **D50** (5000 K, the graphic-arts standard). **Correlated color temperature (CCT)** characterizes a light by the temperature of the theoretical blackbody radiator with a similar color appearance: average daylight ~6500 K, direct sunlight ~5500 K, overcast skylight over 10,000 K, incandescent lamps ~2850 K (*Measuring Colour*).

### [[Natural Daylight Variation & Hyperspectral Scene Data|Light/Natural Daylight Variation & Hyperspectral Scene Data]]
Daylight is physically dynamic. **Rayleigh scattering** — atmospheric molecules scatter short (blue) wavelengths far more strongly than long (red) ones — makes the sky blue; at sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels a thicker atmospheric layer so blue is scattered away and only red/orange reach the observer. Physical light data is also captured as hyperspectral datasets — complete SPDs at 1 nm or 5 nm intervals from 380 to 780 nm, giving every surface a unique spectral "fingerprint" — the raw material for colorimetric and gamut analysis (*Illusions of Seeing*, *Contemporary Color*, *Munsell Color Science Lab*).

### [[Spectral Locus & Excitation Purity|Light/Spectral Locus & Excitation Purity]]
In CIE color space, the **spectral locus** is the horseshoe-shaped outer boundary marking the positions of all **monochromatic, pure spectral lights** from about 380 to 700 nm. **Excitation purity** is a physical measure of saturation: the ratio of a color's distance from the achromatic white point to its total distance from the spectral locus, often given as **100a/(a+w)** — the fraction of monochromatic light in a white-plus-spectral mixture (*Color for Science, Art, and Technology*).
