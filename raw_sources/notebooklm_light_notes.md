The following summary details the physical nature of light as described in the sources, strictly excluding biological or perceptual processes.

### 1. Fundamental Nature of Light: Electromagnetic Radiation and Duality
Light is a form of **electromagnetic radiation**, specifically coupled electric and magnetic fields that oscillate transversally (perpendicularly) to their direction of travel. 

*   **Wave-Particle Dualism:** Light possesses properties of both waves and particles. In the wave model, it is a vibrating disturbance spread in space. In the particle model, it consists of discrete, indivisible packets of energy called **photons** or corpuscles.
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "We can describe light as electromagnetic waves with color identified by its wavelength. We can also consider light as a stream of minute packets of energy called photons, which create a pulsating electromagnetic disturbance."
    *   **"color-for-science-art-and-technology.pdf":** "quantum theory, explains the equivalence of the particle and wave characteristics of light... A photon or quantum may appear to act in two apparently very different ways."
*   **Universal Speed:** In a vacuum, light travels at a constant universal speed ($c$) of approximately **300,000 kilometers per second** or 186,000 miles per second.
    *   **"vision-science-photons-to-pheno":** "Photons radiate outward from their source... at the enormous speed of 186,000 miles per second."
    *   **"Illusions of Seeing":** "This wave propagates from the source with the very high speed of light, constant for the vacuum, of approximately 300,000 km/s."

### 2. Physical Properties: Wavelength, Frequency, and Energy
Light is physically quantified by its wavelength, frequency, and photon energy.

*   **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** The physical distance between one wave crest and the next, typically measured in **nanometers (nm)** ($10^{-9}$ meters) for the visible region.
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "The wavelength is the distance from one wave crest to the next, and is represented by the Greek letter lambda, $\lambda$."
*   **Frequency ($f$ or $\nu$):** The number of waves passing a stationary observer per second, measured in **Hertz (Hz)**. 
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "Moving waves can be described by their frequency, or number of waves per second... the range of the pure spectrum perceived by the eye extends from about $4.3 \times 10^{14}$ Hz in the red range to about $7.5 \times 10^{14}$ Hz in the violet."
*   **Quantization of Energy ($E$):** According to Planck’s Law, the energy of a photon is directly proportional to its frequency and inversely proportional to its wavelength ($E = hf$). In a vacuum, energy in electron volts (eV) relates to wavelength as **$E(eV) = 1240 / \lambda(nm)$**.
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "There is an inverse relation between the energy $E$ of the photon and the wavelength... In terms of wavelength in nanometers (nm) and energy $E$ in electron volts (eV)... $E (eV) = 1240 / \lambda (nm)$."
*   **Physical Amplitude:** The height of the light wave determines its physical intensity or flux (the number of photons passing through a unit area in a unit time).
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "The intensity or brightness of the light is defined by the flux, which is the number of photons passing through a unit area in a unit time."

### 3. The Visible Spectrum and Prismatic Dispersion
The visible spectrum is a narrow window within the broader electromagnetic spectrum, which includes everything from radio waves (meters long) to X-rays and gamma rays (nanometers and smaller).

*   **Spectral Range:** Sources define the visible band as approximately **380–400 nm up to 700–780 nm**.
    *   **"vision-science-photons-to-pheno":** "In fact, the human visual system is sensitive only to photons with wavelengths between about 400 and 700 nanometers."
    *   **"The Measurement of Color":** "Visible light, called the visible spectrum, is that portion of the electromagnetic spectrum having wavelengths from about 380 nm... to 780 nm."
*   **Prismatic Dispersion:** Sir Isaac Newton demonstrated that "white" sunlight is a heterogeneous mixture of component wavelengths. Passing light through a prism causes **refraction**, where different wavelengths bend at different angles, separating the beam into its constituent spectral hues.
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "Newton showed experimentally that a beam of white light, when passed through a glass prism, is decomposed into a spectrum consisting of a large number of colors... these colors are components, not modifications, of white light."
*   **Nonspectral Lights:** Certain lights, such as **purple or magenta**, do not have a single corresponding monochromatic wavelength; they are physical mixtures of red and violet wavelengths.
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "No single wavelength exists for the color purple. Purple can be created with a mixture of wavelengths in both the red and the violet."

### 4. Emission vs. Reflection
Light interaction with objects is categorized by whether the energy is directly generated or bounced off a surface.

*   **Emission (Radiant Light):** Light directly emitted from an energy source such as the sun, chemical fire, or electrical filaments.
    *   **"Contemporary Color":** "All bulbs or lamps are sources of radiant light, which means that the light is directly emitted from an energy source."
    *   **Incandescence (Blackbody Radiation):** When solid objects are heated, they emit a predictable spectrum of light following Planck’s blackbody equation. The peak wavelength ($\lambda_m$) of this radiation shifts to shorter wavelengths as temperature ($T$) increases, a relationship known as **Wien's Law** ($\lambda_m = 2897/T$).
*   **Reflection:** Light waves that bounce off an object rather than being absorbed or transmitted.
    *   **Specular Reflection:** Occurs on smooth, polished surfaces where light reflects in a single direction; the **angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection**.
    *   **Diffuse Reflection:** Occurs on rough surfaces where light penetrates slightly, bounces between particles, and is scattered in many different directions.
    *   **"vision-science-photons-to-pheno":** "If the surface is highly polished, or specular... the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection... On the other hand, if the surface is dull or matte... the light is scattered diffusely in many directions."

### 5. Interactions with Matter: Absorption, Refraction, and Scattering
*   **Absorption:** Matter selectively removes specific wavelengths from the incident light. The energy of the photon is transferred to an electron, which is then excited to a higher energy state; the "lost" energy often appears as heat.
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "A photon transfers all of its energy to the absorbing pigment and is 'lost,' absorbed, that is, from the light beam."
*   **Refraction:** The bending of light as it passes between media of different densities and refractive indices (N), calculated by the ratio $N = \sin(i)/\sin(r)$.
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "Refraction refers to the change in direction of light resulting from the change in the velocity as light passes from one medium into another."
*   **Fluorescence:** A physical process where a substance absorbs high-energy (short wavelength) photons and re-emits them as lower-energy (longer wavelength) photons.
    *   **"The Science of Paintings":** "The absorption of light photons of high energy and reemission of photons of lower energy... is called fluorescence."

### 6. Illuminants and Spectral Power Distributions (SPD)
A light source is physically defined by its **Spectral Power Distribution (SPD)** curve, which plots the amount of power emitted at each individual wavelength.

*   **Illuminants:** These are the mathematical descriptions of SPDs used as international standards for colorimetric calculations. 
    *   **"The Measurement of Color":** "An illuminant is a mathematical description of a light source. Illuminants may describe light sources that do not actually exist in the laboratory."
*   **Standard Illuminant Data:**
    *   **Illuminant A:** Represents incandescent tungsten lighting at a color temperature of 2854 K.
    *   **Illuminant C:** A mathematical simulation of average daylight produced by filtering Source A.
    *   **Illuminant D65:** Represents "average" daylight with a color temperature of 6500 K; it is more accurate than C because it includes proper ultraviolet energy.
    *   **Illuminant D50:** A standard for the graphic arts industry at 5000 K.

### 7. Correlated Color Temperature (CCT)
CCT characterizes a light source by comparing it to the temperature (in Kelvin) of a theoretical **blackbody radiator** (or full radiator) that would emit light of a similar color.

*   **Physical Variation:** 
    *   **Average Daylight:** ~6500 K.
    *   **Direct Sunlight:** ~5500 K.
    *   **Overcast Skylight:** Can exceed 10,000 K.
    *   **Incandescent Lamp:** ~2850 K.
    *   **"The Measurement of Color":** "A block of carbon... simulates a full (blackbody) radiator... Light sources approximate this response and are often described by the temperature of the full radiator having the same (or closest) color."

### 8. Spectral Locus and Excitation Purity
These terms describe boundaries and ratios within the physical color space defined by the CIE.

*   **Spectral Locus:** On a CIE chromaticity diagram, this is the horseshoe-shaped outer boundary that represents the position of all **monochromatic, pure spectral lights** from ~380 nm to 700 nm. 
    *   **"color-for-science-art-and-technology.pdf":** "Here the saturated (pure, most intense) colors of the spectrum occur on the curved line extending from red to violet."
*   **Excitation Purity:** A physical measure of saturation, representing the ratio of a specific light mixture's distance from the **achromatic (white) point** to its total distance from the spectral locus. 
    *   **"color-for-science-art-and-technology.pdf":** "When B is at the central white point W... the percent saturation or purity is then given by $100a/(a + w)$."

### 9. Natural Daylight Variation and Atmospheric Physics
Daylight is physically dynamic, changing its spectral character based on the sun's altitude and atmospheric conditions.

*   **Rayleigh Scattering:** The molecules of the atmosphere scatter shorter wavelengths (blue) much more intensely than longer ones (red). This is the physical cause of the blue sky.
    *   **"Illusions of Seeing":** "Lord Rayleigh could show that there is a connection between the wavelength of light and the intensity of scattering... Blue light is therefore scattered much more strongly than red light!"
*   **Sunset/Sunrise Physics:** Because sunlight must travel through a much thicker layer of atmosphere at the horizon, blue wavelengths are scattered away, leaving only the longest red wavelengths to reach the observer.
    *   **"Contemporary Color":** "Sunlight... is not yellow or orange unless the blue wavelengths are filtered out at sunset."

### 10. Hyperspectral Scene and Instrument Data
Physical light measurements can be recorded as granular datasets providing a unique spectral "fingerprint."

*   **Spectral Data Intervals:** Full sets of physical data are often provided at **1 nm or 5 nm intervals** from 380 nm to 780 nm.
    *   **"Munsell Color Science Lab":** "Full set of 1nm data, including all of the following: Illuminant A, Illuminant D65..."
*   **Instrument Evaluation Database:** Data for handheld and benchtop hemispherical instruments are used to calculate radiometric measurements of light sources and displays.
    *   **"The Measurement of Color":** "Spectroradiometers and radiometric colorimeters are used to measure lamps and self-luminous displays... Figure 15.10 gives examples of emission spectra of the three phosphors of a shadow mask tube."
