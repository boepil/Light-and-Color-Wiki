### The Chain from Light to Perception
Color is not a property of objects but a **multi-stage transformation** that converts physical electromagnetic energy into a subjective psychological experience. The full sequence runs **light ג†’ pigment ג†’ eye ג†’ brain**, and each stage sets hard constraints on the next:

### 1. [[Light/index|Light]] ג€” the physical stimulus
- Visible light is **electromagnetic radiation** carried by discrete **[[Light/Wave Nature|photons]]**. Wavelength is inversely proportional to photon energy: ~400 nm photons are high-energy violet/blue, ~700 nm photons are lower-energy orange/red.
- "White" sunlight is a **balanced mixture of all visible wavelengths**; a prism disperses it into the continuous array of spectral hues ג€” the [[Light/The Visible Spectrum|visible spectrum]].

### 2. [[Pigments/index|Pigments]] ג€” interaction with matter
- Pigments are particulate solids that color by **selectively absorbing and reflecting** wavelengths ג€” the **[[Intersections/Optical vs. Physical Mixture|subtractive]]** mechanism. Every added pigment absorbs more light; mixing the three subtractive primaries (cyan, magenta, yellow) ideally yields **black**.
- **Green supremacy:** green pigments can reach high saturation and high lightness simultaneously because they sit at the peak of the human [[Intersections/Color Matching Functions and the Photopic Luminosity Function|luminosity function]] (~555 nm), forcing reds to trade lightness for purity ([[Colors/Why Material Reality Favors Green Over Red|Why Material Reality Favors Green Over Red]], Chs. 2 & 4.1).

### 3. [[Eye/index|Eye]] ג€” biological transduction
- The retina splits the signal into **Rods (~120 million)**, for scotopic (low-light) vision without color, and **Cones (~6 million)**, for photopic (daylight) vision with color and detail ג€” see [[Eye/Rods vs. Cones - Density & Distribution|Rods vs. Cones]].
- Because a single cone obeys the **principle of univariance** (its signal confounds intensity and wavelength), color is encoded by the **activation ratio across the S/M/L cone triplet** ג€” the heart of [[Eye/Wavelength Perception|wavelength perception]].

### 4. neural processing and perception
- Receptor-level **trichromacy** is recombined in neural pathways into **[[Eye/Opponent-Process Color Coding|opponent pairs]]** ג€” redג€“green, blueג€“yellow, blackג€“white. Simultaneous contrast and afterimages arise from lateral inhibition and neural fatigue in these channels; **color constancy** works by discounting the illuminant; **metamerism** follows from reducing full spectra to three cone values ג€” see [[Intersections/Simultaneous Contrast, Color Constancy, Afterimages, Subtractive vs. Additive Mixing|Simultaneous Contrast, Color Constancy & Afterimages]] and [[Intersections/Natural Light Gamut vs. Pigment Gamut - Metamerism|Metamerism]].

### 5. cortical integration and plasticity
- Color is integrated in a specialized cortical network (V1ג€“V4). The circuits are **neuroplastic** ג€” training and language can "rewire" color space ג€” and **memory color** biases perception, so a gray-scale banana can appear slightly yellowish ([[Intersections/The Neuroscience Behind Why Colours Rewire Your Brain|The Neuroscience Behind Why Colours "Rewire" Your Brain]]).

## Handprint Perspectives
Bruce MacEvoy grounds the chain in the same observer machinery, but from the painter's choice: he emphasizes the **retinal trichromatic primaries** are only a "bridging" device, not the primaries of color appearance ג€” appearance is instead governed by the opponent functions along the violet red/blue green (a+/aג€“) and yellow/blue violet (b+/bג€“) axes. He also notes that the **lightness at which each hue reaches peak chroma** varies dramatically around the hue circle, roughly matching the photopic sensitivity function, with the prominent exception of violet: violetג€“violet red hues are spectral mixtures and appear brighter than spectral blue violet *(Source: [[raw_sources/handprint/color18a.md|color18a.html]])*.
