**Scope:** Landing page explaining how the four main sections ([[Light/index.md|Light]], [[Eye/index.md|Eye]], [[Colors/index.md|Colors]], [[Painting/index.md|Painting]]) fit into a single causal chain — from physical photons, through the subtractive limits of pigments, the biological transduction of the retina, and the constructive processing of the brain — plus a suggested reading order.

### The Chain from Light to Perception
Color is not a property of objects but a **multi-stage transformation** that converts physical electromagnetic energy into a subjective psychological experience (*The Science of Paintings*, *The Art of Color*). The full sequence runs **light → pigment → eye → brain**, and each stage sets hard constraints on the next:

### 1. [[Light/index|Light]] — the physical stimulus
- Visible light is **electromagnetic radiation** carried by discrete **[[Light/Wave Nature|photons]]**. Wavelength is inversely proportional to photon energy: ~400 nm photons are high-energy violet/blue, ~700 nm photons are lower-energy orange/red (*The Science of Paintings*, pp. 51–53, 104).
- "White" sunlight is a **balanced mixture of all visible wavelengths**; a prism disperses it into the continuous array of spectral hues — the [[Light/The Visible Spectrum|visible spectrum]] (*Contemporary Color*, pp. 5, 15).

### 2. [[Pigments/index|Pigments]] — interaction with matter
- Pigments are particulate solids that color by **selectively absorbing and reflecting** wavelengths — the **[[Intersections/Optical vs. Physical Mixture|subtractive]]** mechanism. Every added pigment absorbs more light; mixing the three subtractive primaries (cyan, magenta, yellow) ideally yields **black** (*A Comprehensive Overview of Color Vision Mechanisms, Color Spaces, and the Munsell System*, Ch. 4).
- **Green supremacy:** green pigments can reach high saturation and high lightness simultaneously because they sit at the peak of the human [[Intersections/Color Matching Functions and the Photopic Luminosity Function|luminosity function]] (~555 nm), forcing reds to trade lightness for purity ([[Colors/Why Material Reality Favors Green Over Red|Why Material Reality Favors Green Over Red]], Chs. 2 & 4.1).

### 3. [[Eye/index|Eye]] — biological transduction
- The retina splits the signal into **Rods (~120 million)**, for scotopic (low-light) vision without color, and **Cones (~6 million)**, for photopic (daylight) vision with color and detail — see [[Eye/Rods vs. Cones - Density & Distribution|Rods vs. Cones]] (*Illusions of Seeing*, pp. 5, 102, 111).
- Because a single cone obeys the **principle of univariance** (its signal confounds intensity and wavelength), color is encoded by the **activation ratio across the S/M/L cone triplet** — the heart of [[Eye/Wavelength Perception|wavelength perception]] (*Illusions of Seeing*, pp. 102, 111).

### 4. [[Intersections/index|Intersections]] — neural processing and perception
- Receptor-level **trichromacy** is recombined in neural pathways into **[[Eye/Opponent-Process Color Coding|opponent pairs]]** — red–green, blue–yellow, black–white. Simultaneous contrast and afterimages arise from lateral inhibition and neural fatigue in these channels; **color constancy** works by discounting the illuminant; **metamerism** follows from reducing full spectra to three cone values — see [[Intersections/Simultaneous Contrast, Color Constancy, Afterimages, Subtractive vs. Additive Mixing|Simultaneous Contrast, Color Constancy & Afterimages]] and [[Intersections/Natural Light Gamut vs. Pigment Gamut - Metamerism|Metamerism]] (*The Art of Color*, pp. 76, 87).

### 5. [[Intersections/index|Intersections]] — cortical integration and plasticity
- Color is integrated in a specialized cortical network (V1–V4). The circuits are **neuroplastic** — training and language can "rewire" color space — and **memory color** biases perception, so a gray-scale banana can appear slightly yellowish ([[Intersections/The Neuroscience Behind Why Colours Rewire Your Brain|The Neuroscience Behind Why Colours "Rewire" Your Brain]]).

### Suggested reading order
1. **[[Light/index.md|Light]]** — understand the physical stimulus (wavelength, energy, spectral power) as the starting point.
2. **[[Eye/index.md|Eye]]** — the transduction machinery: rods/cones, univariance, opponent coding.
3. **[[Colors/index.md|Colors]]** — the measurement, standardization of human chromatic response: [[Colors/CIE Systems|CIE Systems]] and [[Colors/Munsell Notation|Munsell]].
4. **[[Painting/index.md|Painting]]** — where material limits (pigments) meet perception in practice.

## Handprint Perspectives
Bruce MacEvoy grounds the chain in the same observer machinery, but from the painter's choice: he emphasizes the **retinal trichromatic primaries** are only a "bridging" device, not the primaries of color appearance — appearance is instead governed by the opponent functions along the violet red/blue green (a+/a–) and yellow/blue violet (b+/b–) axes. He also notes that the **lightness at which each hue reaches peak chroma** varies dramatically around the hue circle, roughly matching the photopic sensitivity function, with the prominent exception of violet: violet–violet red hues are spectral mixtures and appear brighter than spectral blue violet *(Source: [[raw_sources/handprint/color18a.md|color18a.html]])*.
