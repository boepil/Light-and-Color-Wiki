## Synthesized Content

*   **MacAdam Ellipses:** These represent regions on the CIE chromaticity diagram where colors are visually indistinguishable from a central color; any color falling within the ellipse is perceived as an identical match to the human eye (**color-for-science-art-and-technology.pdf**, **Contemporary Color**). Their varying sizes across the diagram highlight the **lack of visual uniformity** in the original CIE xy space (**color-for-science-art-and-technology.pdf**).
*   **Optimal Color Solid (MacAdam Limits):** These are the theoretical physical boundaries for non-fluorescent surface colors (**Why Material Reality Favors Green Over Red...**). These limits describe "optimal colors" with step-function reflectance spectra (0% or 100%) (**A Comprehensive Overview...**, **Why Material Reality Favors Green Over Red...**). The resulting solid is **asymmetric**: because the human luminosity function peaks in the green-yellow region (555 nm), green hues can achieve much higher chroma at high lightness levels than red hues can (**Why Material Reality Favors Green Over Red...**).

## Handprint Perspectives

Handprint uses MacAdam ellipses to illustrate a fundamental flaw in traditional, symmetrical color wheels. The ellipses mathematically prove that human color discrimination is highly uneven—we can detect minute shifts in blue-green hues, while our discrimination in the yellow-green region is much less precise. 

This biological reality, rooted in our [[Anatomy]], means that any color model attempting to represent perceptual uniformity (like [[CIE Systems]]) must necessarily warp and distort the color space, proving that a geometrically perfect circle cannot accurately represent human color vision. *(Source: [[raw_sources/handprint/color18a.md|color18a.html]])*

> [!TIP]
> **Note:** While models attempting to correct for these ellipses (like CIELAB) achieve *perceptual uniformity*, Handprint notes that this property does not grant them *mixture predictability* for physical paints. See [[CIE Systems]] for details.
