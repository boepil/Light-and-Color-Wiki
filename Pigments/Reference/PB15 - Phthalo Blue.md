**Scope:** Phthalo Blue (PB15) — copper phthalocyanine blue, the modern palette's intense cyan that replaced Prussian blue, with extreme tinctorial strength and two rival crystal forms.

### Identity

- **Colour Index:** PB15; **copper phthalocyanine**, a macrocyclic aromatic pigment (tetrabenzotetraazaporphin) whose sharp absorption edge gives it extreme chromatic purity.
- **Crystal variants:** the **alpha form (PB15, 15:1, 15:2)** is a clean, bright red-shade blue but is the least stable "meta" form — it can shift green under strong solvents or heat; the **beta form (PB15:3, 15:4)** is the green-shade ("peacock") blue and the most stable crystal form, resistant to recrystallization ([[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt1.md|pigmt1.html]]).

### History

- The phthalocyanine class was first described **1927–1928 by chemists at Scottish Dyes Ltd.**, discovered accidentally as a dark impurity where a dye intermediate reacted with a metal vessel; the blue was first marketed **1936** and entered artist paint in the years after.

### Production

- Made by reacting phthalonitrile with copper salts; the crude pigment is brought to usable strength and crystal form by **mechanical attrition or acid swelling** — dissolving in concentrated sulfuric acid and precipitating by dilution with ice water.

### Color Data

- **Hue:** ranges from bright reddish-blue to greenish "cyan"; at full strength the masstone is so deep it can appear blackish with a coppery bronze sheen — the true character appears in the undertone when spread thinly or reduced with white.
- **Transparent, with enormous tinctorial strength** — usually "let down" with 50–75% inert base (alumina hydrate) to be manageable on the palette ([[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt1.md|pigmt1.html]]).
- **Highly staining** — described as getting "into everything"; stains paper fibers deeply and is very hard to lift ([[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt8.md|pigmt8.html]]).
- **Permanence:** remarkably permanent and lightfast in all media, approaching the stability of inorganic pigments.
- **Handling:** in oil it dries rapidly but can form "stringy" pastes with poor plasticity, often stabilized with wax; in watercolor it stains the paper.
- **Munsell notation:** not established in this corpus for PB15.

> [!WARNING] **Strengths to be managed (source-confirmed)**
> - Extreme tinting strength means a drop of PB15 overpowers a mixture — it is the classic "sparing" palette pigment (handprint).
> - Because it is chemically identical across industries, the same PB15 grade appears in house paints, automotive finishes, and plastics — artists must buy artist-grade lightfast formulations ([[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt1.md|pigmt1.html]]).

### Relation to Art and Painters

- **Replaced [[PB27 - Prussian Blue|Prussian blue]]** as the standard intense cyan of the modern palette; the essential **cyan primary of the CMY subtractive triad**, and the basis of "chromatic black" mixtures with **[[PR83 - Alizarin Crimson|alizarin]]**/**[[PR122 - Quinacridone Magenta|quinacridone]]** and **[[PV23 - Dioxazine Violet|dioxazine violet]]**.

## Handprint Perspectives

MacEvoy treats phthalo blue as one of the indispensable "backbone" pigments — but warns it overwhelms. His doctrine: plan phthalo mixtures before the brush touches paper, let it down, and use it for extreme darks and high-chroma passages rather than general washes. He notes the beta (PB15:3) form is the one artists want for stability, and that the same chemistry is mass-produced for industry, so pigment quality varies by brand grade *(Source: [[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt1.md|pigmt1.html]], [[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt3.md|pigmt3.html]])*.
