**Scope:** Cadmium Red (PR108) — cadmium sulfoselenide, the 20th-century replacement for vermilion, with warning-level handling notes.

### Identity

- **Colour Index:** PR108; **cadmium sulfoselenide, CdS·xCdSe** — a solid solution in which selenium replaces a fraction of the sulfur in the cadmium sulfide lattice (*Artists' Pigments*).
- Hue — from light red to deep maroon — is set by the **sulfur-to-selenium ratio** (*Artists' Pigments*, *The Science of Paintings*).

### History

- Cadmium salts were discovered in **1817**, but the red is a modern addition: **commercialized ~1910**, introduced as an artist's color **in America around 1919**.
- It "has largely replaced genuine vermilion in the modern palette due to its superior lightfastness and reliability" (*Artists' Pigments*, *Contemporary Color*).

### Production

Produced by **coprecipitating cadmium and selenium compounds**, then **calcining at 700–800°C in an inert atmosphere** to develop the final red shade (*Artists' Pigments*).

### Color Data

- **Extreme opacity and brilliance; "absolutely lightfast and permanent in all techniques"** (*Artists' Pigments*, *The Science of Paintings*).
- **Mixing:** frequently used with **viridian** to create deep, resonant blacks or "broken" hues (*A Comprehensive Overview...*).
- **Munsell notation:** not established in this corpus for PR108.

> [!WARNING] **Toxicity flag**
> Warning for **toxic dust and vapors** — heated or inhaled during manufacture; powders must not be ground or sanded casually. It is, however, chemically stable in the paint film (*Artists' Pigments*, *Ralph Mayer*).

### Relation to Art and Painters

- Became the stable high-chroma red of the 20th century — a non-fugitive alternative to "aniline" dyes — serving movements that demanded intense, permanent reds. On the modern palette it holds the vermilion niche (the red anchor with cadmium yellow and ultramarine) — see [[Pigments|the section hub]] and the mixing triangles in [[Pigments/The artist's color wheel|Pigment Color Wheel]].

## Handprint Perspectives

MacEvoy's cadmium reds (PR108) are the red anchor of both his full palette and his "primary triangle" doctrine, chosen because they hold hue and chroma better than alizarin under most conditions. He pairs PR108 with ultramarine and cadmium yellow as the strongest opaque mixing set, and — like the corpus — uses the cadmium red + viridian combination for deep portraits and neutral darks. On cost and safety he is direct: cadmiums are the most expense-heavy pigments in the palette and should be handled as powders with care *(Source: [[raw_sources/handprint/palette1.md|palette1.html]], [[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt3.md|pigmt3.html]])*.
