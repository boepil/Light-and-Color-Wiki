**Scope:** How paints are classified as transparent, semi-transparent, semi-opaque, or opaque; the label vocabulary (masstone, undertone, tinting strength); and the complete decoding of everything printed on a tube of artist paint — Color Index names, constitution numbers, series numbers, and lightfastness ratings.

### The Physics of Transparency and Opacity

Whether a paint film hides or transmits depends on the pigment particles suspended in the binder (*The Science of Paintings*, *A Comprehensive Overview*, *Particle Size-Tinting-Polymorphism*):

- **Refractive index difference (Δn):** opacity is primarily the difference in refractive index between pigment and binder. Ultramarine (n ≈ 1.50) in linseed oil (n ≈ 1.48) is transparent — indices nearly match, light passes with minimal scattering; titanium white (n ≈ 2.71) in the same oil is intensely opaque. The vehicle matters too: water (1.33) vs. oil (1.48) changes the Δn and with it the perceived transparency.
- **Particle size:** scattering is most effective when particles are roughly the size of visible-light wavelengths (~500 nm). Large crystals can be transparent; ground to the ~500 nm range they become highly opaque.
- **Painter's use of the classes:**
  - **Transparent** paints — glazed in thin washes over dry color to "sway" or vivify the color beneath (*Carlson's Guide*, *Plein Air Techniques*).
  - **Semi-transparent / semi-opaque** — the largest workhorse class: they both tint and veil.
  - **Opaque** paints — for scumbling (a thin layer over dry paint to gray a color cast), for covering dark passages, and for the final light accents; multiple passes are needed when painting opaque color over dark grounds (*Plein Air Techniques*).

### Masstone, Undertone, Tinting Strength

- **Masstone (mass color):** the color of a thick, undiluted layer that completely hides the surface — alizarin crimson's deep maroon masstone vs. its ruby-red undertone.
- **Undertone:** the color revealed when the pigment is spread thinly or viewed in transmitted light; it is often the more saturated color, and the one that governs tinted mixtures.
- **Tinting strength:** the power of a pigment to influence another color (usually white) in a mixture. Tested by "rub-outs": a weighed pigment (e.g., 0.1 g) mulled with white (e.g., 2.0 g zinc oxide); for extremely strong modern organics like phthalocyanine blue the reduction ratio is extended to **50:1** to disclose tonal character (*Ralph Mayer*, *Particle Size-Tinting-Polymorphism*).

### Reading the Tube: The Anatomy of a Paint Label

Standardized labeling follows the **CS98-62 Paint Standard** and **ASTM D4236** (health labeling) (*The Science of Paintings*, *Ralph Mayer*; handprint pigmt6):

- **Pigment common name** — "Cadmium Red", "Ultramarine Blue".
- **Color Index generic name** — the international identifier, e.g. PR108 = Pigment Red 108, PB29 = Pigment Blue 29.
- **Color Index constitution number** — a five-digit number (e.g., 77196 for PR108, 77007 for PB29) designating chemical structure; withheld when a manufacturer's recipe is proprietary. Numbers 77000–77999 cover inorganics.
- **Series number** — a manufacturer's pricing band: low series for cheap earths, high series for costly cadmiums and cobalts.
- **Lightfastness rating** — the ASTM category (I "Excellent", II "Very Good", III, IV) or the manufacturer's stars; the industrial **Blue Wool scale** runs 1 (fugitive) to 8 (extremely lightfast) with exposure-time equivalents per level (*The Science of Paintings*, handprint pigmt6).
- **Health warning / ACMI seal** — certification that the product meets the Art and Creative Materials Institute safety standard (ASTM D4236) (*Ralph Mayer*).

### The Color Index Naming Structure

- A letter prefix gives the general category (P = pigment), then a hue-family letter: **R** red, **Y** yellow, **B** blue, **G** green, **V** violet, **O** orange, **W** white, **Br** brown, **Bk** black — followed by a number: PR101, PY35, PB29, PG7, PV23...
- **Generic name vs. constitution number:** the generic name (PB29) identifies the pigment's application type; the constitution number (77007) identifies its chemistry. Two chemically different pigments never share a generic name; the same chemistry may have several generic names when it serves different applications (*The Science of Paintings*, handprint pigmt6).
- Suffixes refine variants: **PB15:3** (beta, green-shade phthalo) vs. PB15:6 (red-shade); PY35 vs. PY35:1 (cadmium zinc sulfide lemon).

### Which Lightfastness Rating to Trust

- The **ASTM** ratings are the most rigorous benchmarks, derived from months of exposure to direct southern sunlight; the **CS98-62 Paint Standard** and ASTM D01.57 define the categories.
- **Manufacturer ratings** may be based on the pigment maker's data, chemical reasoning, or tests of a different paint form — not always on the tube's actual paint (*handprint litetest/pigmt6*).
- **Handprint's own verdict:** published lightfastness ratings — including ASTM's — are systematically unreliable: the same generic pigment can receive very different ratings across brands; dilution changes fade rate; tests average brand differences away. MacEvoy rates paints from his own 1999/2004 sunlight exposures and urges artists to treat every published rating as advisory only *(Source: [[raw_sources/handprint/litetest.md|litetest.html]], [[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt6.md|pigmt6.html]], [[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt8.md|pigmt8.html]])*.

### Opacity, Granulation, and Staining Symbols

- **Opacity symbols** printed on tubes/brochures: filled square = opaque, half-filled = semi-opaque, empty square = transparent (some lines use filled/empty circles the same way).
- **Granulation** marks a pigment whose coarse particles settle into paper valleys, producing texture (viridian, ultramarine); **staining** marks pigments that penetrate paper fibers and cannot be lifted (phthalos, quinacridones) — both appear as small icons or letters on modern labels (*handprint pigmt3*, *intstud*).

## Handprint Perspectives

MacEvoy's paint guides are built on exactly the information this page decodes: every pigment evaluation he publishes begins with a one-line behavior summary — "pyrrole red PR254 is a very lightfast, semiopaque, highly staining, dark valued, intense medium red pigment" — combining opacity, staining, value, and strength in a single sentence. He is scathing about the label's trustworthiness, however: many reputable brands omit pigment info or lightfastness ratings entirely, and he requires "conforms to ASTM-D5067" labeling assurance before he trusts any rating. His opacity tests use an indelible black line under the paint film — the difference between painted and unpainted line is the true measure of hiding *(Source: [[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt8.md|pigmt8.html]], [[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt7.md|pigmt7.html]], [[raw_sources/handprint/intstud.md|intstud.html]])*.
