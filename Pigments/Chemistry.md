**Scope:** The chemistry of painters' pigments — why pigments are crystalline solids, how inorganic and organic colorants absorb light, and the reactions that make them fade.

### What a Pigment Is

Pigments are "finely divided, colored particulate solids that are typically insoluble in the medium in which they are used." Unlike dyes — which dissolve into a substrate — "pigments retain their crystal or particulate structure throughout the coloration process" and are meant to be physically and chemically unaffected by the binder (*A Comprehensive Overview..., Color for Science, Art and Technology*).

### Inorganic vs. Organic Structure

- **Inorganic pigments:** mineral compounds — metal oxides, sulfides, sulfoselenides — colored by **ligand field effects** (unpaired electrons in the d or f orbitals of transition metals — iron, cobalt, cadmium — excited by specific wavelengths) or **charge transfer** (Prussian blue: electrons moving between iron ions in different valence states) (*A Comprehensive Overview..., The Science of Paintings*).
- **Organic pigments:** aromatic compounds with **chromophore groups** (e.g., the azo group −N=N−) and **conjugated systems** — alternating single/double bonds making "a diffuse cloud of pi-bonded electrons." Color arises from **π→π\* transitions** (*A Comprehensive Overview...*).
- **High-performance organics** — **phthalocyanines** (macrocyclic) and **quinacridones** (heterocyclic) — owe their intensity to electronic structures with "exceptionally sharp absorption edges" (*A Comprehensive Overview..., The Material Supremacy of Green Chroma*).

### Chroma and Spectral Reflectance

- **Chroma = spectral purity:** how sharply a pigment reflects one narrow band while suppressing all else; a "perfect" pigment "reflects 100% of a specific band and 0% of all other light" (*A Comprehensive Overview...*).
- **Why greens beat reds:** organic green (pigment green-shaded) is "chemically easier to engineer" as a narrow transmission "window in the green spectral region than to isolate a sharp 'spike' in the deep red region, where electronic transitions tend to be broader" (*The Material Supremacy of Green Chroma*, *Why Material Reality Favors Green Over Red*).

### Lightfastness Chemistry

- **Fading is a chemical change, not disappearance** — often "triggered by ultraviolet radiation" (*The Science of Paintings*).
- **Photo-oxidation:** UV/blue photons excite double bonds in the conjugated system, enabling reaction with atmospheric oxygen — converting double to single bonds, "breaking the conjugated framework and destroying the color" (*A Comprehensive Overview..., The Science of Paintings*).
- **Photoreduction:** photons excite substrate molecules (e.g., canvas), adding hydrogen across the pigment's double bonds — likewise breaking the color system (*A Comprehensive Overview...*).
- **Incompatibility reactions:** cadmium sulfide (cadmium yellow) mixed with lead pigments (lead white, chrome yellow) or copper pigments, with moisture, forms **black lead sulfide (PbS)** or copper sulfide — dark brown/gray discoloration (*Color for Science, Art and Technology*, *Artists' Pigments*).
- **Binder interactions:** zinc oxide reacts with linseed-oil fatty acids to form **zinc soaps** that embrittle and crack the film; lead white forms lead carboxylates in oil (*Artists' Pigments*).

## Handprint Perspectives

MacEvoy's paint chemistry doctrine: the historical arc "from natural, inorganic earths to highly engineered synthetic organics (like phthalocyanines and quinacridones)" is the defining material change in modern palettes, and the new synthetics "behave entirely differently in mixtures than historical pigments" — transparency, tinting strength, and lightfastness "require a modern approach to color mixing," rendering "many traditional color theory recipes obsolete" in favor of measured spectral behavior. His permanence chapter reads as the cautionary twin of the chemistry here: the 1888 Russell–Abney *Report on the Action of Light on Water Colours* documented systematic fading, leading to the modern lightfastness standards that synthetic pigments were engineered to meet *(Source: [[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt1.md|pigmt1.html]], [[raw_sources/handprint/pigmt6.md|pigmt6.html]])*.
