### What this section is about

Painters did not always treat color the same way. Early masters tried to **match what things looked like**. Impressionists chased **how light changes color from moment to moment**. Post-Impressionists and Fauves used color to **express feeling**, not just appearance. Modern painters often treated color as **the subject itself** ג€” large fields of hue with no landscape or figure required.

**This section follows that journey** through individual movements and painters, from Vermeer's quiet Dutch interiors to Riley's eye-tricking Op Art. Each page below goes deep on one chapter; this hub only explains what each one is *for* and where to start.

### The pages, one by one

#### [[Newton's Influence on Painters]] ג€” where the color wheel came from

Before the movements, there was a scientific shift: Newton showed that white light splits into a spectrum, and painters inherited the **circular color wheel** that replaced older light-to-dark ladders. This page traces how *Opticks* (1704) fed every later theory ג€” from Moses Harris's symmetrical wheel to Chevreul, Rood, and Munsell ג€” and how Goethe pushed back with a rival, subjective account of color. Start here if you want the **theory backstory** before the painters.

#### [[Vermeer-Dutch Golden Age]] ג€” color in service of quiet realism

In the Dutch Golden Age, color served **naturalistic illusion** ג€” matching textures, organizing rooms around blue-and-yellow light notes, and building luminous whites and soft edges. Vermeer's ultramarine and ochre palette anticipated later optical color theories without abandoning the goal of believable space. If you want the **earliest chapter** ג€” color as careful observation ג€” start here.

#### [[Romanticism-Turner]] ג€” color as atmosphere and emotion

Turner turned color into **weather, fire, and feeling** ג€” veils of cool tone over distant forms, storms of light that dissolve solid objects. He broke academic rules about warm shadows and local color, treating hue as the language of the sublime rather than a report of surfaces. The bridge from descriptive painting to **expressive atmosphere**.

#### [[Impressionism]] ג€” capturing fleeting light outdoors

The Impressionists painted **en plein air**, chasing the hour-by-hour shift of sunlight with broken color and complementary shadows instead of black. Monet's haystack and cathedral **series** ג€” many canvases of the same subject at different times ג€” made color a record of light itself, not an inherent property of objects. The most famous entry point if you wonder **"why did painters stop mixing mud on the palette?"**

#### [[Neo-Impressionism-Pointillism]] ג€” dots of pure color mixed in the eye

Seurat placed **tiny touches of unmixed pigment** side by side so the viewer's eye blended them ג€” optical mixture instead of palette mud. Fed by Chevreul's contrast laws and Rood's spinning-disc experiments, Pointillism tried to **reconstruct light scientifically** on the canvas. Essential if you want to understand why dots of blue and yellow can read brighter than the same colors stirred together.

#### [[Post-Impressionism]] ג€” structure, symbol, and the language of the soul

Van Gogh, Gauguin, and Cֳ©zanne pushed past Impressionism's moment-to-moment light. Color became **symbol, structure, and inner life** ג€” complementary pairs as emotional force, flat symbolic hues, warm-and-cool modulations that build solid form. The pivot from **recording light** to **using color deliberately**.

#### [[Fauvism]] ג€” color freed from what things "really" look like

The "wild beasts" of 1905 placed **arbitrary, high-chroma color** on trees, faces, and skies ג€” not to describe nature but for dramatic impact. Matisse's flat areas and his famous green line showed that a canvas could find its own color logic. The moment color is **fully liberated from local realism**.

#### [[Bauhaus]] ג€” color taught as a formal system

At the Bauhaus, Itten, Kandinsky, Klee, and later Albers treated color as an **autonomous design element** ג€” contrasts, spheres, color-to-form assignments, and Albers's lesson that color **deceives continually** depending on its neighbor. A schoolroom chapter: color as **curriculum**, not just expression.

#### [[Color Field]] ג€” color as the whole subject

Rothko, Newman, and Reinhardt stripped painting down to **large fields of closely related hue** ג€” soft edges, wall-scale canvases, layered glazes that seem to glow from within. Color is no longer describing anything; it **is** the experience. For anyone who asks **"when did color become the painting?"**

#### [[Op Art]] ג€” color that moves because your eye moves

Riley and others built precise patterns that **exploit how the visual system works** ג€” vibrating edges, moirֳ© flicker, afterimages, colors that seem to shift as you look. Color as **physiological event**, not pigment on a subject. The chapter where painting meets **Eye** section science directly.

### How the pieces fit together
