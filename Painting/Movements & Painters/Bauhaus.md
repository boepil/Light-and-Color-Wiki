**Scope:** The Bauhaus and its color pedagogy — the Vorkurs, Itten's seven contrasts and color sphere, Kandinsky's synesthetic color-music, Klee's keystone gray, and Albers's experimental divergence.

### Color as an Autonomous Formal Element

At the Bauhaus color was treated as an **autonomous formal element** interacting dynamically with form and human perception, taught through the **Vorkurs (preliminary course)** — originally aimed at investigating color "as an independent element in a 'vacuum', stripped of its traditional associations with imagery or function" (*Color in Art*, *Contemporary Color*). Bleicher rates the school "probably the most influential of all of the various groups on contemporary art and design" (*Contemporary Color*).

### Johannes Itten's Pedagogy

- **The seven contrasts:** hue, light-dark, cold-warm, complementary, simultaneous, saturation, and extension (*The Art of Color*).
- **12-hue color circle** and a **color sphere** — "a symmetric solid adopted from Philipp Otto Runge" — to visualize the totality of the color universe (*The Art of Color*, *Color in Art*).
- **Subjective color** ("subjective timbre"): every student "possesses a personal, inherent preference for specific color combinations that serve as a key to their individual mode of thinking and feeling" (*The Art of Color*).
- **Spiritual framing:** while objective principles exist, the "deepest and truest secrets" of color are "beheld by the heart alone" (*The Art of Color*, *A Comprehensive Overview...*).

### Wassily Kandinsky's Doctrine

- **Synesthetic color-music:** color as a universal "language of the soul" bypassing cultural associations, with fixed **color–form coordinates**: **yellow = triangle** (aggressive/earthly), **red = square** (stable/matter), **blue = circle** (spiritual/concentric) (*Color in Art*).
- ***On the Spiritual in Art:*** colors set up "vibrations" in the soul, hues acting like musical instruments — vermilion red as "loud beats of the drum" (*Color in Art*, *A Comprehensive Overview...*).
- Labeled a **"pseudo-synaesthete"**: "impossible to verify if his multiple sensory responses were truly involuntary or artistically constructed" (*A Comprehensive Overview...*).

### Paul Klee's Approach

- Color as **melody and movement**, with an "uncommon breadth" of color resources that avoided reduction to a single expressive character (*Color in Art*).
- While Itten ran the Vorkurs, Klee lectured on the **"problems of form"**; his teaching notes (including the **Wassily album**) treated the color wheel and its **middle-point gray** as the "neutral center" or balancing **"keystone"** of the whole system, "where all color tensions find equilibrium" (*Color in Art*, *A Comprehensive Overview...*).

### Josef Albers's Divergence

- Itten's former student, Albers "moved away from mystical systems toward a focus on the **perceptual relativity of color**" (*Contemporary Color*).
- ***Interaction of Color:*** "in visual perception a color is almost never seen as it really is"; "**color deceives continually**"; color is "the most relative medium in art," its "action" depending entirely on its neighbors (*Interaction of Color*).
- **Method:** to eliminate "self-expression" and the physical handling of paint, Albers's exercises used **pre-colored paper chips (color-aid papers)** and silk-screened plates to demonstrate how one hue can appear radically different on different grounds (*Interaction of Color*, *Contemporary Color*).

## Handprint Perspectives

MacEvoy's verdict on the Bauhaus theorists runs on three threads. First, the **hue-only shorthand**: when the "color crank Johannes Itten announces that 'yellow against purple provides a very large contrast between light and dark'" he is visualizing saturated hues alone, ignoring that dark valued yellows are blackish and light valued purples are whitish — the classic traditional-color-theory equation of "color" with saturated hue. Second, **Albers as the exception**: traditional color theory "has almost entirely ignored spatial effects in visual color — the studies of simulated transparency by Joseph Albers being one of the notable exceptions," and the genre-defining "squares within squares" format (a ≥1° square inside a ≥3× field) descends from Chevreul through the post-Bauhaus tradition to Albers's iconic paintings. Third, the **universal-psychology debunk**: Bauhaus designers themselves sent out survey questionnaires seeking consistent emotional color associations, with "hundreds of publications" showing none — "chromatic Esperanto" that cultures reject; Kandinsky's red was "spiritual" only because of the red-cornered icon rooms of rural Russia. Itten's and Kandinsky's "spiritual or 'moral' qualities" attributed to primaries figure among the claims MacEvoy dismisses as "misleading generalizations or crackpot nonsense" *(Source: [[raw_sources/handprint/color18a.md|color18a.html]], [[raw_sources/handprint/color18b.md|color18b.html]], [[raw_sources/handprint/color13.md|color13.html]])*.
